Food Check

Scan a barcode. Get a straight answer about what is in it.

Download for Android

Version 3.1 · 12 MB · Android 7.0 or newer

This is a beta.

That means it works and is used every day, but it is still being changed, and you may find something wrong with it. It updates itself, so a fix reaches you without you doing anything.

It is not medical advice, and it will not always have your product: no free food database has everything, particularly own-brand and very new items.

What it does

  • Tells you whether something is worth eating every day, now and then, or rarely.
  • Explains why, in plain words, and says how much is actually too much.
  • Checks the barcode against food recalls.
  • Also reads skincare, makeup and pet food, not just groceries.
  • Keeps a daily total against limits worked out from your own height, weight and age.
  • Works without a signal for anything you have scanned before.

Installing it

  1. Tap the download button. Your phone will ask if you are sure, choose Download anyway.
  2. Open the downloaded file. Android will say it cannot install apps from this source yet. Tap Settings, turn the switch on, then press back.
  3. Tap Install. Play Protect may say it does not recognise the app, which only means it did not come from the Play Store. Choose Install anyway.
Updating later. The app checks for new versions by itself and offers them to you. You can also come back here and install the newest version straight over the old one. Your daily log and your settings are kept either way.

Your data

There is no account and no sign up, and nothing you scan is sent anywhere we control. Your log, your profile and your limits stay on your phone and are never uploaded. Scanning looks a barcode up at Open Food Facts and the U.S. FDA, so those services see the barcode, and product photos load from Open Food Facts as you view them.
Food Check tells you what is in packaged food and how it compares to ordinary daily limits. It is not medical advice. If you have a health condition, your doctor comes first. Food data from Open Food Facts, used under the ODbL.
